1/**
2 * @file watchdog.hpp
3 * @brief Background health monitor for the pooled database connections.
4 * @details This header declares WatchDog, a small class that owns a background thread. The
5 * thread wakes once per interval, leases one pooled connection through a
6 * DatabaseHanndler, checks whether the link is alive, and reconnects it (and the rest
7 * of the idle pool) when it is not. An atomic flag lets the destructor signal shutdown
8 * and then join the thread cleanly.
9 *
10 * WHY THIS FILE MATTERS IN THE SERVER:
11 * Databases restart and network links drop; without monitoring, a stalled connection would
12 * keep failing requests until somebody manually restarted the server. The watchdog runs in the
13 * background at startup and silently repairs connections, cooperating with
14 * DatabaseConnectionPool::reconnect_all(). It is the self-healing part of the synchronous
15 * database path: controllers do not need to know that a link died and came back.
16 *
17 * CONCEPTS TO LEARN (for beginners):
18 * - std::thread (a real background thread of execution): https://en.cppreference.com/w/cpp/thread/thread
19 * - std::atomic (a flag readable/writable from two threads without races): https://en.cppreference.com/w/cpp/atomic/atomic
20 * - join() (wait for a thread to finish before destroying its owner): https://en.cppreference.com/w/cpp/thread/thread/join
21 * - RAII (the destructor stops and joins the thread): https://en.cppreference.com/w/cpp/language/raii
22 * - std::chrono::seconds (a time duration): https://en.cppreference.com/w/cpp/chrono/duration
23 *
24 * @author Amr Osman <dr3mro@gmail.com>
25 * @date 2026-11-16
26 */
